So generally there are two primary reasons to use strided convolution.
The first one is when you just want high-level features from your image and you don't want low-level features.
Low-level features are called fine features.
In an image, it is very fine.
If your stride value is low, then you can capture those fine features.
If you start increasing your stride value, then your CNN will not capture those fine features, low-level features.
will capture only the high level features, so it can happen that you are working on a computer vision problem
where you need only high level features on the image, in that case you would not use the value of stride equal to 1
you would increase it to 2 or 3, and the second reason is computing power
you have to do a lot of training on a big data set, then you will increase the value of stride so that the training becomes fast.
Obviously because if the value of stride is less, then that training will take more time.
Although this second reason is not that important now because as the computing power has increased,
now people generally train their model by keeping the value of stride as 1.
So honestly strides are not as important as they were 5-10 years ago but in today's date also in certain problems you use strides.
